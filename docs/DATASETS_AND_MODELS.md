# SkillNet India — datasets and models: what we used, and why

## 1. Datasets

| # | Dataset | Type | Size | Used for | Why this one |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **India data & analytics job postings, 2025** (LinkedIn + Indeed; GitHub mirror `AlokTheDataGuy/Data-Science-Jobs-Analytics`) | Real | 8,524 rows (7,674 geocoded) | Demand snapshot, NLP validation, skill co-occurrence | The only recent Indian postings with full job-description text that were reachable. They ship with the author's own keyword labels, so we can test our extractor against an independent reference. |
| 2 | **Multi-sector job postings, Jan 2021 – Oct 2026** | Synthetic | 181,835 | Demand across 14 sectors; 70-month history for forecasting | Real scraped data covers IT/analytics and only a few months. Gap analysis needs blue-collar sectors (solar, nursing, welding, textiles…), and forecasting needs years of monthly history. Generated to the Naukri schema (title, JD text, key-skill tags, city, salary, experience) with role skill bundles, state affinities, trends, seasonality and policy shocks. |
| 3 | **Workforce sample in PLFS person-level format** | Synthetic | 426,639 persons | Supply: who is employed in which NCO occupation, who is jobseeking, their education, technical education and vocational training field | PLFS is the right source (the official labour survey, with NCO-2015 codes and training fields), but the MoSPI microdata portal needs a login and was unreachable from here. The sample uses PLFS's exact fields and code lists (`State, Sector, Sex, Age, General_Education_Level, Technical_Education_Level, Vocational_Training, Field_of_Training, Principal_Status_Code, NCO_2015_3d, Multiplier`), so the real `cperv1` file drops straight in. |
| 4 | **ITIs and polytechnics** | Synthetic | 18,605 (14.8k ITIs, 3.8k polytechnics) | Training infrastructure map, annual trained output (part of supply) | Real DGT trade names and trade-to-skill mapping; counts close to national totals; points placed inside real district polygons. |
| 5 | **District boundaries** (Census-2011 codes, incl. Telangana and Ladakh; `udit-001/india-maps-data`) | Real | 724 districts | The map, district drill-down, placing centres | Current state/UT set and clean GeoJSON. |
| 6 | **Census 2011 state, district and city population and coordinates** (`recurze/IndianCities`) | Real | 36 states, 738 districts, 523 cities | Geocoding postings (city → district), per-capita normalisation, sizing the workforce | Official population base; gives every posting a district. |
| 7 | **Skill taxonomy + NCO-2015 + DGT trades + PLFS code lists** (`pipeline/reference.py`) | Reference (hand-built) | 132 skills, ~520 aliases, 40 NCO groups, 24 ITI trades | The NLP vocabulary and the bridge between demand and supply | No public Indian skill taxonomy maps recruiter language → NCO occupations → training routes (NSQF/ITI/NPTEL). This file is that bridge, and it is editable. |

**Your three datasets.** Kaggle "Indian Job Market 2025-26" and "Job Listing Dataset" go in `data/raw/jobs/` (columns are auto-detected). The PLFS file goes in `data/raw/plfs/`. Rerun `python -m pipeline.run_all` and they replace tables 2 and 3. Kaggle, Hugging Face and the government portals were blocked from the build environment, which is why tables 2–4 are synthetic.

## 2. Models

| Step | Model | Result | Why this model |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Skill extraction (NLP)** | Taxonomy regex matcher (one compiled pattern over 132 skills and ~520 aliases) + RapidFuzz tag normaliser + sentence-level cache | Precision 0.968, recall 0.966, F1 0.967 (40k postings with known skills). Recovers 98.5% of the real dataset's own keyword labels | Fast (~190k postings in about 3 min on 2 CPUs), explainable, and every match maps to a canonical skill with an NCO prior and a training route. Most remaining "errors" are skills implied by the job title, which arguably count as hits. A transformer NER would need labelled Indian job data that doesn't exist yet. |
| **Emerging-skill discovery** | Unmatched recruiter tags ranked by growth in share of postings (last 6 months vs prior 12) | Found 7 of the 8 planted new terms (Agentic AI, Vector Databases, LangChain, Battery Recycling, Green Building, Digital Twin, Heat Pump); the 8th was correctly absorbed into Surveying | Catches skills before anyone adds them to a taxonomy, which is useful for curriculum review. |
| **Skill2Vec** | Word2Vec skip-gram (48-d) on each posting's skill set | 132 skills embedded | Learns which skills travel together; orders each student's learning path (start next to what you know) and finds adjacent skills. Trains offline on our own data. |
| **Occupation bridge** | TF-IDF (word 1-2g + char 2-4g on title, plus extracted skills) → multinomial logistic regression, 40 NCO-2015 groups | 46.5% accuracy on **job families never seen in training** (the honest figure); 100% on a random split only because titles repeat | Maps postings into the same occupation codes PLFS uses. This is the step that makes demand and supply comparable. Linear, fast, inspectable; good enough for state aggregates. |
| **Supply model** | Weighted PLFS aggregation: employed stock = Σ weight × P(skill \| NCO group), with P learned from postings and smoothed toward the taxonomy prior; jobseeker skills from education / technical education / training field; plus ITI output | — | Uses PLFS the way it is designed (state-level, weighted), not at district level where it isn't reliable. |
| **Skill Gap Index** | SGI = 100 × (openings − available)/(openings + available), with evidence levels (postings per cell) | National: +18; tightest sectors Green Energy (+70), Electronics/Semiconductors (+65) | Bounded −100…+100, symmetric, comparable across states and skills. Low-evidence cells are flagged and down-weighted. |
| **Demand forecasting** | Compared **seasonal naive**, **ETS/Holt-Winters**, **XGBoost global model** and **GRU global model (PyTorch)**, plus an ensemble, on a 12-month holdout across 317 series | MASE: ETS 0.818 ✓, ensemble 0.820, XGBoost 0.829, GRU 0.866, naive 1.041 | The original plan was LSTM/GRU, so we tested it rather than assumed it. With about 70 months per series the classic ETS model narrowly wins; the global models do best on noisy state × sector series. The pipeline picks the winner automatically, so with more real history the GRU can take over. Prediction intervals come from holdout errors. |
| **Salary premium** | Ridge hedonic regression: log(salary) ~ role + state + experience + time + skills | Recovers planted premiums (r = 0.83, mean error 1.5 points) | Gives each skill's pay premium within the same role, so a training course's "worth" isn't confused with the role it's attached to. |
| **Priority index** | Transparent weighted sum of 6 normalised components: shortage size, gap severity, demand growth, wage premium, training gap, youth unemployment | — | Policymakers must be able to see why a district/skill ranks first and change the weights. A black-box ranker would fail that test. |
| **Budget optimiser** | Linear programme (SciPy/HiGHS): maximise priority-weighted placements subject to the budget, no training beyond the shortage, a per-programme cohort cap, a 4-week minimum course and optional state capacity caps. The UI uses the equivalent greedy solution | ₹500 Cr → about 4.2 lakh expected placements across 49 programmes in the default run | Optimal, fast, and the constraints read as policy rules. |
| **What-if simulator** | Deterministic projection: openings grow with the forecast, supply grows 3%/yr, trainees × completion × placement arrive after the course | — | Every assumption is a slider, so the result is auditable. |
| **AI Skill Assistant** | Offline intent + entity-linking engine (uses the same taxonomy regexes) in the browser; optional LLM agent (Anthropic or OpenAI-compatible) with 7 data tools on the backend | Every answer lists its sources | The assistant can't invent numbers: it only answers from tools over SkillNet's data. |

## 3. Honest limitations
- Results in this build illustrate the method; they are not findings, because tables 2–4 are synthetic.
- Online postings under-represent informal work even when real.
- The occupation bridge is about 47% accurate on unseen job families: fine for aggregates, not for individual postings.
